## Your Task

Write a news article based on this research that is:

1. **Factual**: Every claim must be supported by verification in the research package
2. **Non-sensational**: Avoid hype, exaggeration, emotional language, and clickbait
3. **Clear and Accessible**: General readers should understand it without oversimplification
4. **Balanced**: Present multiple perspectives when they exist in the research
5. **Appropriate Length**: 600-1000 words unless complexity demands more

## Structure Your Article With

**HEADLINE**: Clear, informative, factual. No clickbait. A reader should know exactly what the story is about.

**LEAD PARAGRAPH**: Answer the key questions:
- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Who is involved?
- Why does it matter?

**CONTINUOUS NARRATIVE**: Weave everything together as a flowing story:
- Begin with the most critical information
- Naturally incorporate supporting details and context as the story unfolds
- Integrate expert perspectives, data, and statistics into the narrative flow
- Weave timeline information into the story as you recount events
- Present conflicting viewpoints as part of the narrative, not as separate sections
- Use smooth transitions between ideas—no jarring breaks or topic shifts
- End with forward-looking perspective, next steps, or broader significance

**CRITICAL**: Write as a continuous narrative. NO bullet points. NO numbered lists. NO section breaks. Everything should flow together as cohesive paragraphs.

## Writing Guidelines

- **Write as a story, not a report**: Information should flow naturally, not be listed
- **Use inverted pyramid structure**: Most important information first, but integrated smoothly
- **Attribute claims**: Cite sources from the research (experts, officials, documents)
- **Be specific**: Use concrete numbers, dates, names from verified research
- **Acknowledge uncertainty**: If something is disputed or unverified, say so
- **Avoid jargon**: Explain technical terms or concepts
- **Active voice**: "The study found" not "It was found by the study"
- **Vary paragraph length**: Some paragraphs 2-4 sentences, others longer when developing complex ideas
- **Smooth transitions**: Connect ideas so readers never feel jolted by topic changes
- **Integrate data naturally**: Weave statistics into sentences, don't present them as isolated facts
- **Embed chronology**: Describe the sequence of events as part of the narrative, not as a separate timeline

## What to Emphasize

**Prioritize**:
- Verified claims over disputed ones
- Concrete facts over speculation
- Primary sources (official statements, court documents) over secondary reports
- Recent developments with current impact
- Human relevance and real-world consequences

**Minimize**:
- Unverified speculation
- Minor details that don't affect the core story
- Background that's not essential for understanding
- Technical details that general readers don't need

## What to Avoid

**DO NOT**:
- Sensationalize or exaggerate any aspect
- Use emotional language or hype
- Make claims not supported by the research
- Present disputed claims as confirmed facts
- Bury the lead or save important information for the end
- Use anonymous sources unless verified in the research
- Inject personal opinion or commentary
- Use clickbait phrases or exaggeration in the headline
- **Use bullet points or numbered lists—write continuous prose only**
- **Break the narrative with section headers like "Background" or "Timeline"**
- **Present information as isolated items—everything must be woven into the story**
