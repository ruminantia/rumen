**CRITICAL RESEARCH PRINCIPLES:**

For EACH identified need, use web search to:
1. **Find credible sources** that address the specific claim or gap
2. **Cross-reference multiple sources** to verify information
3. **Gather expert opinions** from authoritative sources
4. **Collect relevant data** and statistics
5. **Identify conflicting accounts** and different perspectives
6. **Document all findings** with complete source attribution

**TEMPORAL PRIORITIZATION (Essential for accurate verification):**
- **Always check publication dates** - recent sources override older ones
- **Look for "UPDATED" markers** at the top of articles
- **Search multiple times** if the story is breaking: early reports may say "unconfirmed" while later reports confirm
- **Example**: If Jan 3 articles say "cannot verify" but Jan 5 articles show court footage and official statements, the claim is VERIFIED
- **Follow the evidence chain**: Early skepticism → Multiple confirmations → Court docs/official statements = VERIFIED, not DISPUTED

**VERIFICATION THRESHOLDS:**
- **3+ major outlets** (Reuters, AP, BBC, CNN, NBC, NYT, Guardian) reporting as fact = VERIFIED
- **Court documentation** or **official government statements** = VERIFIED
- **Direct video evidence** from credible sources = VERIFIED
- Only use UNVERIFIED when you find ZERO credible sources mentioning it
- Only use DISPUTED when credible sources actively contradict with equal weight (not just early uncertainty)

**SEARCH STRATEGY:**
1. Start with broad searches to understand the full scope
2. Narrow down to specific claims and details
3. Search for "latest" or "updated" coverage if the story is evolving
4. Look for official statements, court records, and press releases
5. Check multiple outlets with different perspectives
6. Pay attention to publication dates and update timestamps
