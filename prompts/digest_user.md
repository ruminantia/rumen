## ARTICLE STRUCTURE

Your article should follow this structure:

### 1. Compelling Headline
Create a clear, informative headline that captures the essence of the story.

### 2. Lead Paragraph (The "Lede")
- Open with the most newsworthy information
- Answer: Who, what, when, where, why (the 5 W's)
- Hook the reader with significance or drama

### 3. Nut Graph
- Early paragraphs (2-3) explain the broader context
- Why this story matters now
- What's at stake

### 4. Body Paragraphs
**Weave together elements from the research package:**

- **Verified Claims** (Section 1): Your factual backbone
- **Expert Opinions** (Section 3): Authoritative voices
- **Statistics & Data** (Section 5): Concrete evidence
- **Historical/Political/Economic Context** (Section 4): Background
- **Timeline** (Section 6): Chronological narrative
- **Key Players** (Section 7): Who's involved

**Organize by:**
- Thematic sections (not just matching research sections)
- Narrative flow that tells a story

### 5. Addressing Contention
**For Conflicting Reports & Perspectives** (Section 2):
- Present both sides fairly
- Use attribution: "Some experts argue..." / "However, other analysts point out..."

**Draw from Historical & Contextual Background** (Section 4):
- Help readers understand the bigger picture

### 7. Strong Conclusion

## WRITING GUIDELINES

### Tone and Voice
- **Professional but accessible**: Write like a respected news outlet (NPR, Reuters, AP)
- **Objective**: Present facts without taking sides
- **Engaging**: Use active verbs, specific details, compelling quotes
- **Authoritative**: Demonstrate mastery of the material

### Attribution Style
**Preferred** (narrative flow):
- "According to a report by the Financial Times..."
- "Legal experts say the proposal raises constitutional questions."
- "Statistics from the FBI show..."

**Direct** (for key facts):
- "China's Ministry of Commerce announced the ban on January 6, 2026."
- "FBI data indicates 52% of hate crimes in 2024 targeted Jews."

### Handling Verification Levels

**VERIFIED** → Present as fact:
- ✅ "Judge Alvin K. Hellerstein will preside over Maduro's arraignment."
- ✅ "China imposed export restrictions on dual-use goods."

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED** → Use qualifying language:
- ✅ "The ban may include drones and rare-earth materials."
- ✅ "Estimates suggest over 1.3 million Jewish constituents."
- ❌ "The ban includes drones." (too definitive)

**DISPUTED** → Attribute to sources:
- ✅ "While Blakeman claims most antisemitism comes from the left, FBI statistics show significant right-wing involvement as well."
- ❌ "Most antisemitism comes from the left." (presenting disputed claim as fact)

**UNVERIFIED** → Omit or explicitly label:
- ✅ Omit from main narrative, or
- ✅ "Claims of specific contracts could not be verified through public records."

### What to INCLUDE
✅ All VERIFIED claims from Section 1
✅ Expert opinions that add insight (Section 3)
✅ Key statistics with context (Section 5)
✅ Relevant historical/political/economic context (Section 4)
✅ Timeline of major events (Section 6)
✅ Key player identities and roles (Section 7)
✅ Multiple perspectives on contentious issues (Section 2)
✅ What's unknown or couldn't be verified (Section 8) - briefly, if newsworthy

### What to EXCLUDE
❌ Original article content (you're writing new, not summarizing)
❌ UNVERIFIED claims (unless crucial and clearly labeled)
❌ Academic citations (use narrative attribution instead)
❌ Repetitive information
❌ Sections that exist only to structure research (this isn't research, it's journalism)
